The practice range

27 September – 10 October · before any release

A greens book came first, with the terrain rebuilt from the brush stamps and checked against heights measured in game. Maps, books and cards followed within days. From 3 October the app would only ever read a course, never change it. The same day, Course Designer Studio replaced the first desktop app.

A place for every course

30 October – 13 November · 2.0 to 2.9

2.0 gave each course a place: a real or invented coastline, ten years of wind, and hole notes written from the course itself. The fortnight that followed added a QA checklist, a hole simulator that plays whole holes, watercolour hole plates, and a new workspace.

Faster, deeper, in 3D

13 – 17 November · 3.0 to 3.9

Twelve releases in five days. The terrain got much faster, the ground could be turned in 3D, and six new map styles took after golf architects' own drawings. Then Studio started reading more of what a designer sets: out of bounds, cameras, the meters, the sun. It also began to judge a course: pin difficulty from the greens, and a whole course difficulty sheet with a suggested handicap.

Hole 4

The physical course

18 – 21 November · 4.0 to 4.2

The latest work makes the maps match what you see in game. Streams built from bunker walls are drawn as walled water. Boxes now move off the holes. And every rock shape was measured in game, using shadows and a sun ladder, so boulders, rock walls and half-buried ridges show at their real size.
